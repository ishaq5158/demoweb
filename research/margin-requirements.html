The two primary factors are market volatility and liquidity

Market volatility measures how significantly the price of an asset can fluctuate. Higher volatility increases the risk of losses, while lower volatility generally indicates more stable market conditions.

Market liquidity refers to how easily an asset can be bought or sold without significantly affecting its price. Higher liquidity makes positions easier to close quickly, while lower liquidity increases the risk of losses during liquidation.

These are the two primary factors that influence Margin Lending Requirements (Margin Rate). Higher volatility and lower liquidity generally lead to higher Margin Lending Requirements (Margin Rate) and lower leverage. Conversely, lower volatility and higher liquidity generally allow for lower Margin Lending Requirements (Margin Rate) and higher leverage.

This is because higher volatility and lower liquidity increase the risk that positions cannot be liquidated quickly, potentially resulting in greater losses.

Other factors may also influence Margin Lending Requirements (Margin Rate), but market volatility and liquidity are among the primary determinants.
